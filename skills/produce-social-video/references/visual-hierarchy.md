# Visual hierarchy doctrine

Apply this doctrine whenever real footage, captions, labels, graphics, and a series watermark share the frame.

## Choose the viewing mode first

Every beat must declare exactly one primary viewing mode:

- `watch`: the viewer is watching a person, event, machine, place, or primary evidence;
- `understand`: the viewer is reading a diagram, calculation, comparison, boundary, or exercise.

Never style a `watch` beat as a slide. Never keep live footage as a decorative inset merely to prove that a source exists.

## Rules for `watch` beats

- Default real footage to edge-to-edge full frame. Crop or reframe deliberately while preserving the subject.
- During source audio, use full-screen footage unless an approved comparison, necessary archival treatment, or simultaneous evidence requires split screen.
- Do not place footage inside a white, light-gray, grid-paper, browser, poster, or presentation canvas.
- Keep permanent overlays subordinate. A source label may appear briefly on first use, then fade out.
- Put Chinese captions in the lower safe area by default. Move them only to avoid a face, essential action, or embedded source text; never move captions to the top merely to fill layout space.
- Use one semantic caption line when practical. Captions must not compete with a headline, source label, and watermark at the same time.

## Rules for `understand` beats

- Use a full-frame diagram or designed explanation only when the relationship cannot be understood efficiently from footage and narration alone.
- Show one claim, mechanism, comparison, or calculation per state.
- Return to footage after the explanation has done its job. Do not preserve a presentation shell across the following footage.
- A bright canvas is allowed for a true diagram; it is not a brightness correction for dark footage.

## Overlay hierarchy

Use this priority order:

1. human face, action, or primary evidence;
2. required caption;
3. temporary explanatory emphasis;
4. source/provenance label;
5. persistent watermark.

If two lower-priority elements reduce the clarity of a higher-priority element, remove or relocate the lower-priority element.

## Watermark visibility

- Use the canonical lower-right `硬核火星人` asset.
- Check it against the actual pixels beneath it at the start, midpoint, and end of every materially different scene style.
- On bright or visually busy footage, use an approved adaptive dark backing/outline treatment that preserves the canonical mark; do not allow pale gold to disappear on white.
- Do not move the watermark to solve contrast unless the brand standard explicitly permits that move.

## Contact-sheet rejection test

Create a contact sheet before the review render and reject the composition when any condition is true:

- a continuous source-audio passage reads as a deck, webpage, poster, or framed presentation rather than a video;
- the same decorative canvas dominates three or more consecutive samples;
- the speaker/evidence is materially smaller than full screen without a functional reason;
- captions sit at the top without avoiding a verified collision;
- the watermark disappears or becomes ambiguous in any sampled scene;
- labels, headlines, and captions create three simultaneous text zones around a speaking subject.

Do not use luminance metrics to override this test. A bright frame can still have poor hierarchy.
