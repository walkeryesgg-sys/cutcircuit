# Source-led commentary template

Use this template for a 4–8 minute public-facing video built from one strong interview, event, vlog, speech, or documentary source when the approved treatment is full-screen footage, Chinese narration, selected source-language audio, and normal subtitles without explanatory cards.

## Viewer promise

Turn access to a rare scene into a transferable insight. Preserve the source as evidence instead of treating it as decorative B-roll.

## Default viewing contract

- Keep real footage full-screen.
- Alternate Chinese narration with complete, verifiable source-audio excerpts.
- Use one restrained subtitle system for narration and translated source speech.
- Add no title cards, labels, or text explanations unless the approved brief asks for them.
- Let narration supply context and interpretation; let original audio supply proof, personality, pauses, and emotion.
- Use the approved native Jianying voice. Do not substitute CosyVoice when the series voice is Jianying.

## Ten-beat story spine

Adapt the beats to the material; preserve their narrative functions.

1. **Contradiction cold open** — begin with a surprising quote, behavior, or consequence.
2. **Access and context** — identify who filmed it, where, when, and why the access is unusual.
3. **Concrete explanation** — show the subject explaining an abstract system through a visible object or decision.
4. **Failure cost** — place breakdown, explosion, rejected maneuver, damage, or uncertainty in the first half.
5. **Iteration response** — show what the team does next and why failure is part of the production rhythm.
6. **Delayed spectacle** — withhold the clean launch, reveal, success, or grand visual until after the midpoint.
7. **Engineering trade-off** — translate the grand goal into mass, time, risk, energy, cost, or another concrete constraint.
8. **Human reaction** — contrast generations, roles, or risk tolerance.
9. **Humanizing turn** — move from the grand subject to games, habits, family, humor, or another revealing detail.
10. **Repeatable conclusion** — answer the cold-open question with one sentence viewers can retell.

Do not force a factual event into the wrong beat. A Starship launch is not Falcon footage; an abort or sea diversion is not an explosion. Label entities and events from verified evidence, never visual resemblance.

## Audio ownership

- Narration owns setup, compression, causal bridges, and interpretation.
- Source audio owns exact claims, revealing exchanges, reactions, and personality evidence.
- Never cut a source speaker mid-sentence.
- Keep source excerpts long enough to preserve the question and answer when context depends on both.
- Normalize every segment to one sample rate and channel layout before assembly.
- Use a single final AAC encode.
- Smooth hard audio boundaries with a 20–40 ms fade while preserving intentional pauses.
- Keep music subordinate to speech and lower it further during source evidence.

## Retention rhythm

- Establish person, action, conflict, or consequence in the first 3 seconds.
- State the episode question and viewer reward within 15–30 seconds.
- Introduce new evidence, interpretation, tension, or emotional movement every 20–40 seconds.
- Put failure or uncertainty before success when the lesson is iteration.
- Delay the most spectacular success image until after 50% of runtime unless it is required for the cold open.
- A later callback may reuse the success image only when it changes function: first as proof, later as conclusion.

## Full-screen image rules

- Prefer native horizontal 16:9 footage for full-screen passages.
- Reject portrait footage padded with side bars for a hero shot, climax, or final image.
- If only portrait evidence exists, use it earlier as evidence, replace it for the emotional payoff, or use an explicitly designed crop/layout approved in the brief.
- End on a horizontal, full-bleed, moving hero image for at least 6 seconds.
- Use **reality → vision** for a grand conclusion when appropriate: real launch/action first, then a full-width future-facing image such as flight, orbit, Moon, or Mars.
- Hold or gently extend the final frame for 0.8–1.2 seconds; never end on black or an unfinished motion.
- Reject embedded subscribe prompts, player controls, unrelated channel titles, third-party watermarks, and visibly soft opening shots.

## Paper-edit fields

For every segment, record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| output beat | narrative function |
| source window | exact source time or asset |
| audio owner | narration or source |
| unique task | the one new thing this segment adds |
| picture reason | why the image proves or advances the line |
| payoff position | first half, after midpoint, or conclusion |

Before rendering, verify that failure and payoff positions match the intended emotional curve.

## Release gates

- First frame has a clear, sharp subject.
- Every source excerpt is transcribed, translated, and semantically tied to adjacent narration.
- Captions cover every spoken phrase and stay inside the safe area.
- No black frames, unintentional freezes, channel-layout switches, missing audio, clipping, or boundary clicks.
- Inspect each media window at start, midpoint, and end.
- Inspect first 3, 15, and 30 seconds plus the final 10 seconds.
- Confirm the final hero shot is horizontal, full-screen, moving, and emotionally larger than the preceding shot.
- Complete a linear watch before claiming 9.5 or higher.
