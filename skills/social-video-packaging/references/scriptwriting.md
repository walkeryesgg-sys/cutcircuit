## 1. Narrative contract

Before writing, complete this sentence:

`The viewer enters believing ___; discovers ___; then realizes ___; and leaves wanting to say/share ___.`

If the four blanks repeat the same idea, the story lacks progression. Build one clear emotional and intellectual journey, not a pile of facts.

When the account has an explicit worldview or persona, also complete:

`An ordinary viewer sees ___; this account sees ___; the factual bridge between them is ___.`

The second view must reinterpret the event and change the story's direction. Merely adding a catchphrase, greeting, Mars reference, or branded ending does not establish a viewpoint.

For a 1,000–1,500 Chinese-character narration, target roughly:

- hook: 8–15%;
- surprise: 25–35%;
- reversal: 30–40%;
- elevation: 15–25%.

These are pacing guides, not mechanical quotas.

## 2. Four-part structure

### Hook — earn the next ten seconds

Start inside a concrete image, conflict, costly decision, dangerous consequence, or counterintuitive claim. Name a familiar entity before an unfamiliar project name. State what is strange and open one answerable gap.

Soon supply a concrete clue, action, or conflict relevant to the question. A scene or choice can advance the story without explaining the answer. Delay the conclusion when the buildup keeps increasing understanding or anticipation, and ultimately deliver the title promise. Do not substitute an early declaration of the author’s position for evidence.

Avoid greetings, channel introductions, topic summaries, long chronology, and “今天带大家了解”. A question mark alone is not a hook.

#### First-three-second hard gate

Write and audit the first three seconds separately. It must contain:

1. a familiar person, company, object, or consequence;
2. a concrete abnormal fact, contradiction, costly choice, or high-stakes change;
3. one answerable gap followed by meaningful clues, action, or conflict; the next beat need not reveal the answer.

Useful structures include “真正厉害的不是 Y，而是 Z”, “大家以为 X，公开信息却指向 Y”, and “X 花了/冒了 ___，真正要解决的却是 ___”. Use structures, not canned wording. Emotion such as “疯了” or “惊呆了” earns no credit unless a verified fact makes that reaction natural. A script fails if its first three seconds are background, a topic announcement, or a dramatic adjective without a concrete reason to continue.

### Account-exclusive analysis — make the account necessary

Every public-facing 硬核火星人 script must contain one account-exclusive analysis. This is an original, defensible interpretation of verified facts—not an exclusive fact claim. Complete:

`Most coverage says ___. 硬核火星人 argues ___. The factual bridge is ___.`

The analysis must change the stakes, mechanism, or meaning of the event and appear early enough to shape the hook or body. Label it as analysis when it is not the source's stated purpose. A fact summary, generic optimism, a Mars catchphrase, or an unsupported prediction does not pass.

### Real disagreement — create evidence-backed conflict

Every public-facing script must identify one real fault line when credible competing judgments exist. Prefer conflicts such as:

- fans' ambition case versus critics' execution-risk case;
- vertical integration and speed versus capital intensity and manufacturing yield;
- short-term economics versus long-term infrastructure;
- the official claim versus a clearly attributed skeptical interpretation.

Represent both sides in their strongest defensible form, then let the account make a clear judgment or ask the audience to decide on a specific criterion. Do not fabricate a critic, borrow an unrelated celebrity as an opponent, hide settled facts to manufacture debate, or assert an algorithmic benefit from comments. If no genuine disagreement exists, use a real decision tradeoff instead of fake controversy.

### Surprise — reward attention

Translate scale into a defensible image. Reveal a specific number, mechanism, decision, or connection that changes the viewer's first estimate of the story.

Use the sequence:

`claim → visual/comparison → consequence → next question`

Use 15–30 seconds as a pacing diagnostic rather than a rigid reveal schedule. A developing scene, consequential choice, new clue, or explanation can reward attention. Each paragraph should advance the story, understanding, or anticipation; it need not answer or pose a question. Allow evidence to accumulate before stating the author’s judgment.

### Reversal — change what the story means

Do not merely add a bigger fact. Replace the provisional interpretation:

- “It looks like X, but the real constraint is Y.”
- “The number is impressive, but that is not the important part.”
- “The apparent advantage is also the biggest risk.”

Use at least one factual correction or boundary when the viral premise is easily misunderstood. Preserve momentum by making the correction itself revealing, not apologetic.

The reversal must be supported by the evidence ledger. Use “plans to”, “claims”, “could”, or “if achieved” when appropriate.

Keep the mechanism invisible in the publishable narration. By default, do not announce structure with phrases such as “第一个反转”, “第二个反转”, “故事反转了”, “接下来是升华”, or their equivalents. Let the next fact invalidate, narrow, or enlarge the viewer's current explanation. Structural labels may appear in outlines and audits only.

Prefer these invisible transitions:

- answer the current question, then reveal why that answer is incomplete;
- introduce a fact that cannot fit the viewer's current explanation;
- reuse an earlier number or image with a different meaning;
- shift from object to system, present to future, advantage to cost, or ambition to physical constraint;
- place two true statements next to each other and let their contradiction create surprise.

For a worldview-led account, use a dual-perspective reversal when it fits the evidence:

1. show the ordinary interpretation;
2. name what that interpretation cannot explain;
3. switch to the account's worldview;
4. support the new interpretation with a public goal, repeated behavior, physical constraint, or consequence;
5. label the final synthesis as analysis when it is not a stated fact.

Example pattern: `From Earth's view, this solves today's supply problem. From a Mars view, it builds the industrial capability required for tomorrow's off-world system.` Do not claim a project is “for Mars” unless a source says so.

### Elevation — create social value

Move from the person or event to a larger human question, decision, identity, or future consequence. Compress rather than summarize. The last 2–4 sentences should provide:

1. a judgment earned by the body;
2. a memorable image or contrast;
3. a reason to share, discuss, follow, or enter the profile that is more meaningful than a bare “点个关注”.

For public-facing 硬核火星人 narration, integrate one homepage-retention CTA from [end-cta.md](end-cta.md) into the final 1–2 sentences. Treat it as the continuation of the story, not housekeeping added after the strongest idea. Skip it for member originals, solemn source excerpts, or an explicit no-CTA request.

## 3. Retention mechanics

Use a beat ledger. For every beat, record:

| Beat | Viewer question | New reward | Open loop | Visual proof | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Reject beats that only repeat mood. In a 1,000–1,500-character script, use 6–10 meaningful beats and at least two mental-model updates.

Useful connectors include “但真正奇怪的是”, “先别急”, “更夸张的还在后面”, “问题来了”, and “到这里，故事突然变了”. Use them sparingly; the following sentence must repay the promise.

Maintain sentence variation. Put the most surprising word late in a sentence when clarity allows. Prefer one vivid analogy over three adjectives. Use rhetorical questions to open loops, not to replace evidence.

## 4. Voice system

For the 硬核火星人 voice:

- speak as an informed, excited guide with a Mars-scale perspective;
- combine industrial detail with an optimistic civilizational horizon;
- use vivid physical images: factory floor, launch window, engine, orbit, power, heat, distance;
- sound conversational and decisive, not institutional;
- allow playful lines and controlled audacity, then return to a verifiable fact;
- make the audience feel smarter after the emotion, not manipulated by it.
- explicitly contrast “地球人的问题” with “火星人的问题” when the topic has a defensible civilization-scale implication;
- treat Earth as the current workshop and Mars/space as the horizon: explain which concrete Earth capability must be accelerated before the larger future becomes possible;
- make the Mars perspective causal, not decorative—the viewpoint must explain a scale, urgency, investment, or design choice that the ordinary interpretation leaves unanswered;
- separate three layers in wording: the documented project purpose, Musk's attributed public vision, and the account's Mars-view synthesis.

Avoid repetitive hype words, fake insider language, unsupported mind-reading, invented motives, biography padding, and forcing every topic into a conspiracy or destiny narrative.

## 5. Evidence-safe drama

Use this principle: **increase narrative tension, never evidentiary certainty.**

For technology stories, build a three-column distinction before using words such as “黑科技”, “独家”, or “颠覆”:

| Layer | Question | Required wording |
|---|---|---|
| system difference | What workflow, architecture, integration, or business model differs from incumbents? | Explain the mechanism and consequence |
| supplied technology | Which process, equipment, IP, or partner technology is planned or licensed? | Attribute it; do not credit it to the project founder |
| demonstrated capability | What has actually run, shipped, reached yield, or entered volume production? | State the achieved milestone and date |

If the project has no demonstrated proprietary breakthrough, say so. The strongest story may be a manufacturing loop, system integration, target product, or operating constraint rather than a secret invention. Never let “planned to use an advanced node” become “has mastered the node.”

Translate technical features into a physical consequence the audience can visualize. Use at most two unfamiliar terms in one spoken beat, and immediately explain why they matter: less power, smaller cooling hardware, faster iteration, higher density, fewer handoffs, or lower mass.

Allowed:

- convert an abstract number into a labeled comparison;
- dramatize a confirmed physical consequence;
- contrast an attributed ambition with the engineering barrier;
- present a clearly marked interpretation after the facts.

Reject:

- a planned project written as completed;
- a campus or total floor area written as one building footprint;
- an inference presented as a private motive or secret plan;
- unsupported superlatives;
- invented dialogue, scenes, causality, or “内部人士” claims;
- emotional language that survives only if the qualifying words are removed.
- visible writing scaffolding in the final narration, including numbered twists or claims that explicitly tell the audience how to feel surprised.
- calling a partner's process technology the founder's invention, or calling a planned feature a demonstrated “black technology.”

## 6. Scoring system

First apply fatal gates. A script cannot pass if it contains a material factual upgrade, a title/body mismatch, a missing promised payoff, fabricated evidence, or an opening dominated by greetings/background. If the user explicitly requests a persona/worldview, also fail scripts where that viewpoint is decorative and does not alter the explanation, stakes, or ending.

For public-facing 硬核火星人 narration, also fail the script when the first three seconds lack a concrete hard hook, the account-exclusive analysis is absent or unsupported, or no genuine disagreement/decision tradeoff is designed.

Then score out of 100:

| Dimension | Weight | Pass condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Hook strength | 15 | concrete tension and clear gap within the first 2–3 spoken sentences |
| Surprise/reward density | 15 | specific rewards recur; no long exposition desert |
| Reversal strength | 15 | at least one supported reinterpretation, not merely another fact |
| Elevation/share value | 10 | ending produces an earned larger meaning and quotable image |
| Progression and open loops | 10 | each section answers and opens; at least two mental-model updates |
| Spoken naturalness and worldview | 10 | readable aloud, varied rhythm, account-native; requested worldview changes the interpretation rather than decorating it |
| Evidence precision | 15 | claims correctly marked as fact, plan, attribution, estimate, or inference |
| Title-cover-opening alignment | 5 | one promise, complementary wording, immediate acknowledgment |
| Information value | 5 | viewer learns mechanisms or consequences, not only mood |

Passing thresholds:

- publishable: at least 85/100, no fatal gate, no dimension below 60% of its weight;
- strong candidate: at least 92/100, no dimension below 70%;
- scores are diagnostic, not proof of virality.

For every score, cite the exact script line or structural beat that earns it. Subtract for unsupported hype, repeated conclusions, empty transitions, jargon clusters, and paragraphs that do not change the viewer's understanding.

## 7. Revision procedure

1. Read the script aloud or simulate spoken cadence.
2. Label each paragraph H, S, R, E, or delete. A paragraph with no job is suspect.
3. Mark every open loop and its payoff. Remove unpaid questions.
4. Circle every number and superlative; verify its comparison dimension and evidence status.
5. Cut the first paragraph if the second starts faster.
6. Replace abstract claims with one physical image or consequence.
7. Strengthen the weakest of surprise or reversal; do not add random facts.
8. Rewrite the final four sentences until the earned conclusion and homepage CTA feel like one continuous thought. Keep the strongest idea intact; do not let a generic CTA become the emotional last word.
9. Score again and save the clean TTS copy separately from headings, citations, and audit notes.

10. For persona-led work, underline the exact factual bridge that earns the worldview shift. If no bridge exists, remove the forced persona angle or mark it explicitly as a metaphorical interpretation.

11. Search the clean narration for structural labels such as “反转”, “升华”, “钩子”, “爆点”, and “故事到这里”. Remove them unless the word is genuinely part of the subject matter.

12. For technology stories, mark each technical claim as system difference, supplied technology, or demonstrated capability. Cut jargon that does not produce a concrete consequence.

## 8. Commercial-explainer patterns learned from high-retention analysis

Use these patterns for long-form explainers, business stories, and entrepreneur
profiles when the source supports them. They are decision criteria, not phrases
to imitate.

### Start from a visible result, then expose the hidden system

The most useful opening sequence is:

`familiar entity or spectacular result → concrete anomaly / price / risk → the hidden business question`

Do not open a SpaceX, platform, or economic story by defining the industry.
Open with something the viewer can already recognize, then show why the obvious
explanation is incomplete. Examples of the shape: a rocket is recovered but
still needs customers; a celebrated creator has revenue but lacks control; a
city looks rich but has a different source of wealth and cost.

### Give the video one explanation engine

Before outlining, state the compact model that every major beat will test. It
may be two necessary conditions, a three-part business equation, or a conflict
between incentives. For example:

`repeatable launch business = cost per mission × turnaround cadence × reliable demand`

Cases, data, and visuals must answer one term, reveal its limitation, or show a
conflict between terms. If a case does none of these, cut it even if it is
interesting.

### Build each explanatory beat as a miniature story

Use:

`viewer question → public evidence / visual proof → plain-language translation → business consequence → stronger next question`

At each major shift, change the viewer's task: understand a mechanism, follow a
person's choice, compare a number, see who benefits, or confront a constraint.
For long videos, avoid leaving the same task untouched for several minutes.
The point is not faster cutting; it is a genuine change in what the viewer gets
to understand.

### Make conflict structural rather than melodramatic

For every business strategy, map at least two of: who gains, who pays, who bears
the risk, who can block it, and what would make the strategy fail. A person can
carry this pressure, but must not become a substitute for the mechanism.

### Fact anchors and account judgment

Use a two-layer voice:

- factual anchors establish dates, numbers, decisions, and demonstrated
  capability;
- account judgment interprets the incentive, pressure, or strategic meaning.

An account judgment may be memorable and emotionally charged, provided its
factual bridge is visible and it is not presented as an internal fact or quoted
motive. The right standard is not “sound neutral”; it is “make the audience
feel the judgment was earned.”

### Packaging role split

The cover, title, and first spoken beat must progress rather than repeat:

- **cover**: the recognisable object plus its visual/commercial tension;
- **title**: the actor and non-obvious question or consequence;
- **opening**: the specific anomaly and the first piece of proof, then the
  deeper question.

Avoid objectless teaser copy and generic crisis words. A claim such as “崩了” or
“资本游戏” earns a place only when the opening immediately identifies what
changed, for whom, and why the phrase is warranted.
