## 1. Visual thesis

Define one sentence before designing:

`The viewer should instantly see ______ and wonder/understand ______.`

Use one dominant subject, one secondary context cue, and one short text idea. A cover is not a synopsis.

Use the rule: **one image, one tension, one visual proof**. The cover must survive as a feed card before it succeeds as a full-resolution design.

Title and cover should divide the work:

- title: names the event, question, or consequence;
- cover: supplies the visual evidence, emotion, scale, or compact concept.

Avoid repeating the exact publishing title on the image.

## 2. Canvas specifications

Unless the user or platform requires another export size, create:

- 16:9 — 1920×1080
- 4:3 — 1600×1200
- 3:4 — 1440×1920

These are production masters, not claims about every platform's immutable upload limit. Export high-quality JPG or PNG as appropriate.

Never stretch artwork between ratios. Recompose from the master assets while preserving geometry.

## 3. Composition rules

- Keep the focal subject recognizable at small-card size.
- Use real imagery when credibility and evidence matter; label concepts/renders when ambiguity is possible.
- Prefer 2–8 Chinese characters for cover copy; exceed only when the phrase remains instantly readable.
- Prefer one text block and one focal subject. Add a second text level only when it changes interpretation rather than decorating the frame.
- Maintain strong figure-ground contrast without crushing shadows or producing sharpening halos.
- Limit typography to two hierarchy levels.
- Keep logos and recurring brand marks in a consistent safe zone.
- Do not place essential words or faces against the outer edges.
- Do not use arrows, circles, shocked faces, flames, or warning colors unless they communicate real information and fit the account.
- Do not fabricate a quote, interface, event, endorsement, or “news screenshot” to simulate evidence.
- When authenticity is central, prefer an authorized real frame or clearly identified render over photorealistic generation. Never make a concept visualization look like documentary proof.

## 4. Ratio-specific direction

### 16:9

- Use cinematic left-right tension or large-scale environment.
- Reserve one side for text and the other for subject when possible.
- Suitable for Bilibili, YouTube, and horizontal share surfaces.

### 4:3

- Tighten horizontal empty space; enlarge the subject relative to 16:9.
- Keep the central thesis intact under modest top/bottom crops.
- Use as the bridge composition for mixed desktop/mobile cards.

### 3:4

- Rebuild vertically: foreground subject above/below contextual scale, not a center crop of 16:9.
- Keep face, key object, and text away from likely top/bottom UI overlays.
- Suitable as the primary concept for mobile card and vertical-first surfaces.

## 5. Legibility and safety checks

For every ratio:

1. View at actual small-card size.
2. Convert mentally or physically to grayscale; verify hierarchy survives.
3. Check bright and dark screen modes where relevant.
4. Verify no face, logo, number, or qualifying word is cropped.
5. Ensure a render cannot be mistaken for current real-world footage.
6. Confirm text remains accurate when seen without the title.
7. Compare all three covers side by side; they must look like one release, not three campaigns.
8. Reduce the image to roughly 10–15% of canvas size; the subject and text must still be identifiable without zooming.
9. Apply a blur/squint test; the intended first focal point must remain dominant.
10. Overlay likely platform UI and crop zones; do not place decisive words, numbers, or faces beneath them.
11. Read cover, title, and opening together; remove exact repetition and check that the cover does not imply more than the video proves.

For YouTube-specific export, follow the current official thumbnail recommendation when practical: 16:9, high resolution, currently 3840×2160 recommended and at least 640 px wide. The shared 1920×1080 master remains a production baseline. Vertical YouTube videos may display an automatically generated 4:5 thumbnail on some mobile surfaces, so verify the live presentation rather than assuming the custom 16:9 image appears everywhere.

## 6. Generation brief

Specify:

- ratio and pixel size;
- dominant subject and source asset;
- factual status of imagery (photo/render/illustration);
- background/environment;
- exact cover copy;
- text position and hierarchy;
- palette and mood;
- brand/watermark placement;
- crop-safe exclusions;
- elements explicitly prohibited;
- thumbnail-size acceptance test.

For every release package, generate or edit and save actual upload-ready image files for all three ratios, then inspect every exported ratio. A written brief, source screenshot, contact sheet, or prompt alone is not an image deliverable.
