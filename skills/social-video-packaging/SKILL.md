# Social Video Packaging

Build a publishable package, not a list of generic clickbait variants. Optimize for the chain:

`qualified impression → chosen view → early retention → sustained watch → satisfaction/share`

Treat the title, cover, first seconds, and description as one promise. Never improve clicks by making the promise less accurate.

## Member original-audio selections

For `member_source_curated` / 会员原声精选, read [references/member-source-publishing.md](references/member-source-publishing.md). Deliver source-grounded publication copy explaining background, actual selected content, significance and a concrete viewer reflection. This mode overrides generic forced reversals, narrated exclusive analysis and public CTAs; keep promise and factual audits.

## Required workflow

1. **Inspect the source.** Read the script or brief. If a video exists, inspect its metadata, representative frames, opening, and key claims. Do not infer the story from a filename alone.
2. **Identify the release context.** Determine platform(s), audience, format, duration, account voice, publication goal, and whether the package is for discovery, search, subscribers, social forwarding, or conversion. Make safe assumptions when unspecified and label them. When WeChat Channels is among the requested platforms, treat it as the primary platform unless the user says otherwise.
3. **Refresh platform rules.** If publishing is imminent or exact limits/features matter, check current official platform documentation. Platform interfaces and limits change; do not rely on remembered numeric limits. Use [platforms.md](references/platforms.md) for stable tendencies and research routing.
4. **Create an evidence ledger.** Separate:
   - confirmed facts;
   - attributed claims or plans;
   - estimates/comparisons;
   - interpretations;
   - unresolved or unsafe claims.
   Reject any title or cover that upgrades a plan into an accomplished fact, a campus into one building, an estimate into a measurement, or a person's claim into consensus.
5. **Find the audience question.** Express the story as one concrete question a non-expert would care about. Prefer a real contradiction, consequence, scale comparison, decision, or hidden cause. See [method.md](references/method.md).
6. **Choose one lead promise.** Rank candidate hooks by audience relevance, surprise, visual proof, evidence strength, distinctiveness, and ability to pay off early. Keep secondary facts for the description or video.
7. **Assign the three surfaces.** Write a one-line packaging contract. The cover makes the object, emotion, or scale visible; the title supplies the contradiction, question, or consequence; the opening acknowledges the promise and soon supplies a concrete clue, action, or conflict that advances it. The final answer or author judgment may be revealed later, after the evidence earns it. If all three repeat the same sentence, redesign the package.
8. **Generate title prototypes.** Produce candidates across at least four distinct structures, including:
   - direct question;
   - statement that creates a question in the viewer's mind;
   - scale plus consequence;
   - apparent contradiction;
   - mechanism or hidden cause.
   Do not create superficial synonym variants.
9. **Score and reject.** Apply the rubric in [method.md]. An evidence failure is disqualifying regardless of click potential. Recommend one primary title and two genuinely different alternates, explaining the tradeoff briefly.
10. **Write for retention when narration is requested.** Read [scriptwriting.md](references/scriptwriting.md). Build the body around `hook → surprise → reversal → elevation`, with early meaningful clues and progressive story development. Use 15–30 seconds as a pacing diagnostic, not a deadline to explain or reveal an answer; a developing scene, action, or consequential choice can also reward attention. For public-facing 硬核火星人 narration, also read [end-cta.md](references/end-cta.md), select one eligible homepage-retention CTA at random, and integrate it into the final 1–2 sentences without weakening the earned ending. Keep claims inside the evidence ledger even when the voice becomes dramatic.
    - Treat the first three seconds as a hard gate. Open with a familiar entity plus a concrete abnormal fact, consequential conflict, costly decision, or evidence-backed counterintuitive claim. Create one answerable information gap and soon provide a relevant clue, action, or conflict. Do not force an immediate conclusion or opening declaration of the account’s position. Reject greetings, topic announcements, background-first openings, and empty reaction formulas such as “这让我惊呆了” when the following fact does not earn the emotion.
    - For every public-facing 硬核火星人 script, state one **account-exclusive analysis**: a distinctive interpretation that changes what the event means, not a summary or an invented exclusive fact. Record the factual bridge that supports it and surface the analysis early enough to influence the hook or main narrative. Without a defensible account-exclusive analysis, the script fails.
    - Design one **real disagreement** when the evidence supports competing judgments. Use a consequential fault line—ambition versus feasibility, speed versus risk, fans' interpretation versus critics' interpretation, or present constraint versus future bet—and invite the audience to choose or reason. Do not invent opposition, misquote critics, leave a factual hole on purpose, or turn speculation into certainty merely to provoke comments.
11. **Adapt by platform.** Do not paste one title and description everywhere. Apply [platforms.md](references/platforms.md) and produce native copy for each requested platform. For WeChat Channels, always read [wechat-channels.md](references/wechat-channels.md) and audit both the autoplay encounter and the forwarded-card encounter.
    - **MuskKnowledgeLab hard gate:** a WeChat Channels short title must contain **fewer than 16 Chinese characters**. Count the exact publishable title, excluding no platform UI text. Put mechanism, attribution, and forwarding context in the cover, opening line, and publishing copy instead of overflowing the short title. Reject the package until the count is shown and passes.
12. **Deliver the cover system.** Create coordinated, upload-ready 16:9, 4:3, and 3:4 **image files** as well as their briefs, using [covers.md](references/covers.md). A brief, screenshot sheet, or prompt alone is not a cover deliverable. Preserve the same visual thesis while recomposing—not stretching—the image. Inspect every exported design at feed-card size, not only full canvas, and save the files beside the publication package.
13. **Run the promise audit.** Verify:
    - title and cover complement rather than repeat each other;
    - opening acknowledges the promised question immediately;
    - the main promise is substantially answered in the video;
    - key entities appear early enough for search and comprehension;
    - description adds context instead of restating the title;
    - sources, attribution, uncertainty, and copyright status are handled.
14. **Run the unified quality gate.** Apply [quality-gate.md](references/quality-gate.md), then the deeper narration audit in [scriptwriting.md](references/scriptwriting.md). Revise every fatal failure and every weak dimension. Do not award points without citing the exact artifact that earns them.
15. **Prepare iteration.** Test materially different theses, not punctuation or synonyms. When analytics are available, diagnose the whole funnel and segment by traffic source where possible. Do not treat CTR alone as the winner. Prefer watch time, retention, satisfaction, or qualified viewing when the platform supports native tests.
16. **Save to the knowledge project.** Unless the user names another destination, save research notes, scripts, publishing copy, audits, and generated covers under the active Obsidian project's production area, using one folder per content item. Keep the original media directory source-only. Never place skill outputs beside downloaded/raw video files merely because the source video lives there.

## Default deliverable

Use [output-template.md](references/output-template.md). Include:

- packaging thesis and intended audience;
- evidence ledger and wording boundaries;
- primary user question;
- hook ranking;
- a clean, TTS-ready narration when requested, plus a separate scored audit;
- one integrated end-of-video homepage CTA for public-facing 硬核火星人 narration, selected from the rotation bank rather than repeated mechanically;
- the exact first-three-second line, one evidence-backed account-exclusive analysis, and one real-disagreement design for every public-facing 硬核火星人 script;
- platform-specific title, cover text, description/caption, hashtags/tags, and CTA;
- 16:9, 4:3, and 3:4 cover briefs with safe crop notes;
- actual, upload-ready 16:9, 4:3, and 3:4 cover image files;
- title-cover-opening promise check;
- A/B alternatives and what each test is designed to learn.

Do not overwhelm the user with dozens of unranked titles. Show the reasoning that changes the choice, then make a clear recommendation.

For the MuskKnowledgeLab workspace, default to `07 Production/<content-name>/`. Keep Markdown and related cover assets together inside that project folder, and link to the external source media rather than copying the source video into Obsidian.

## Quality bars

- Use natural language native to the platform and account; avoid press-release tone, generic AI phrasing, and empty intensifiers such as “震撼”“炸裂”“颠覆” unless the content itself earns them.
- Prefer concrete images, consequential verbs, short spoken sentences, controlled exaggeration, and progressive revelation over exposition-first writing.
- Treat factual safety and cold-start appeal as coequal gates: a truthful script with no immediate reason to keep watching is not a release candidate.
- Make the viewer update their mental model at least twice: the surprise expands the story; the reversal changes what the story means.
- Let emotion rise from facts and consequences. Never turn speculation into certainty merely to achieve a dramatic reversal.
- Put recognizable entities and concrete stakes before unfamiliar project names.
- Prefer an understandable comparison over a contextless large number.
- Use a question only when the audience plausibly cares, the answer is non-obvious, and the video answers it.
- Do not manufacture conflict with a famous person or company merely to borrow search traffic.
- Do not repeat the same claim in title, cover text, and opening sentence verbatim.
- Keep cover copy shorter and more visual than the publishing title.
- Use hashtags selectively for classification and community context, never as a keyword dump.
- Preserve source links and primary attribution in descriptions for research-heavy content.
